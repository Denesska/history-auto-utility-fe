# Main navigation shell

## Functionality

This is the app's main navigation frame — every screen after login sits inside it.

**On desktop** (wide screens), navigation is a permanent left sidebar with:
- App logo/home shortcut and a car search box at the top.
- A short list of global sections: Garage, Documents, Maintenance, Reports, Journal — plus **Admin** (usage stats), shown only to administrator accounts (see `features/admin/FEATURE.md`).
- "My Cars" — an expandable list of the user's cars (owned and shared-with-them); tapping a car expands a sub-menu for that car: Overview, History, Documents, Reports, Plan, Notes, Wishlist (coming soon), Journal, Sharing.
- A notifications/alerts area (invites to shared cars, expiring documents, unread notifications).
- A footer with a shortcut to the combined "All reports" view, the user's profile (name, email, avatar, edit/logout), and the app version.

**On mobile** (phone-width screens), the sidebar is replaced by:
- A bottom tab bar with up to three chosen destinations, followed by a fixed Account tab, plus a floating "+" button in the middle whose action changes depending on the current screen: add a vehicle (Garage), add a maintenance record (History), add a document (Documents), new note (Notes), new wish (Wishlist), new journal entry — for the car you're viewing, if any (Journal), new supplier (Suppliers), new contract (Contracts), or open the car's own "…" add-actions sheet (a single car's hub screen). When the current screen has nothing to create — Reports, Plan, the Account/Settings screen, Organization, Fleet costs, or a screen your role can't add to (e.g. a business role other than an organization admin can't add a car) — the button stays visible but shows **disabled**: greyed out, with no press feedback, and tapping it does nothing. Which destinations appear in the bar, in what order, and — for one tied to a car (Documents, History, Plan, Notes, Wishlist, Journal) — which car each one opens, is a per-account choice made from Settings; until that's changed it defaults to today's Garage, Reports, Journal. See "Mobile bottom bar" in `core/navigation/FEATURE.md` for the full behavior (including single car) and "Bottom bar" in `features/settings/FEATURE.md` for the editor itself.
- Account was moved here (previously it only lived as an icon button in the top header) so it's reachable with one tap from anywhere, without cluttering the bar — Documents gave up its bar slot to make room for it: a document is always added/viewed for one specific car, so its global entry point matters less than being able to check spend/reports across all cars at a glance, which is a genuinely "no car in particular" need. Documents is still reachable from within a car (still there) or from the Account screen (still reachable globally, just one tap further), and is now also one of the choosable bottom-bar destinations.
- With exactly one car in the account, Garage reads "My car" and goes straight to it instead of a list — see "Single car" in `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`.
- The bottom bar (and its Account tab) disappears while looking at a specific car's screens, filling in the "Add vehicle" form, or writing/editing a Journal entry — those screens use the space differently and have their own way back. Account/Settings is reached via the bottom tab bar everywhere else; there's no separate entry point on these screens (see decision below).
- Notifications (car-share invites, access changes, document-expiry reminders, VIN/plate conflicts) live on the Account screen, not here — see `features/settings/FEATURE.md`. The Account tab's icon shows a red badge with the unread count so there's something to notice from any global screen without opening it.

**Per-car navigation**: opening a car switches the chrome into a per-car mode — the bottom tab bar (mobile) or the global section list (desktop) is replaced by that car's own sub-menu (Overview, History, Documents, Reports, Plan, Notes, Sharing, Journal for that car), and the header shows a back button that returns to the exact page you came from (see `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`; with no history it steps up one level of the car's menu). Two exceptions keep the bottom bar (mobile) on screen instead, since that's how you got there: the car's own hub in single-car mode (it's the "My car" bottom-bar item, with no back button there — it's a top-level screen), and any of that car's tabs that's currently one of your chosen bottom-bar items (e.g. Documents, if it's in your bar) — see "Bottom bar visibility on car-scoped routes" in `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`.

**Personal vs. business accounts.** Every session is either *personal* or *business* (chosen at login; see `features/auth/FEATURE.md` and `ACCOUNT-CONTEXT.md`), and the shell adapts to it:
- **Menu:** each menu entry belongs to one or both contexts. Business shows Garage (the company fleet), Documents, Contracts, Maintenance, Reports, Journal, Suppliers and **Organization**; Contracts is hidden for Drivers and Finance. Personal shows the same minus Organization and Suppliers (business-only), plus **Admin** for administrator accounts only (Admin is an app-owner tool and never appears in business). Entries of a vertical feature (Contracts, Suppliers) also disappear when the organization has that feature switched off (see `core/features/FEATURE.md`). Opening an address that doesn't fit the current context or a disabled feature sends you back to the Garage.
- **Business look:** in a business session the accent color changes from orange to a petrol blue, and a permanent badge (building icon, company name, small "Business" label) sits at the top of the sidebar on desktop; tapping it opens the company picker to switch company. On mobile there is no strip at the top of the screen: the Account screen's "Account" card starts with an account-details block instead. In a personal session it shows the name, email and a "Personal account" label; in a business session it shows the company name, a "Business" label, your role and your email, and tapping it switches company. Next to it sits an icon-only logout button (replacing the old text button). A personal session looks as before.
- **Switching to business:** a personal session has a small "Business account" entry (bottom of the sidebar; in the Account screen on mobile). It logs you out and opens the login page on the Business tab.

## Implementation

**Public API: `index.ts`** exports `scopeDataOf`/`ContextScopeData` (re-exported from `context-scope.guard.ts`, which now itself re-exports them — along with `fitsContext` — from `core/context/context-scope.ts`, where the actual scope/role/feature matching logic lives so `core/car-page/car-page-contributions.service.ts` can reuse it without `core/` importing a feature) and `AccountDetailsComponent` — used by `auth/` (the error interceptor reads scope data off the route) and `organization/`/`settings/` respectively. `MainComponent` and `main.routes.ts` stay internal to the shell.

- **Component**: `MainComponent` (`main.component.ts` / `.html` / `.scss`), selector `app-main`, wraps every route under `/main/**` via `<ion-router-outlet>`.
- **Desktop sidebar** items: one `allNavItems` list in `main.component.ts` (Garage, Documents, Sale contract, Maintenance, Reports, Blog, Admin, Organization, Supplier), each carrying the same `scope`/`orgRoles` metadata as its route (`NavItem extends ContextScopeData`); `navItems` is a `computed()` over `SessionContextService` + `FeatureService` using `fitsContext()` (`core/context/context-scope.ts`); an item/route may carry `feature?: FeatureKey` (documents = `documents`, maintenance = `maintenance`, reports = `reports`, blog = `journal`, saleContract = `sale_contract`, supplier = `supplier`) and is hidden/redirected to the garage while that feature is disabled. Admin additionally needs `requiresAdmin` (`isAdmin` signal, from `AdminApiService.isAdmin()` fetched once in `ngOnInit`) and is personal-only; Organization is business-only (no longer driven by `hasOrganization()`, which was removed along with `organizationGuard`); `resolveActiveMenuItem` still maps `/main/admin` to `'admin'`.
- **Route scoping**: every child of `mainRoutes` has `data: { scope: ('personal'|'business')[], orgRoles?: string[] }`; `context-scope.guard.ts` exports `contextScopeChildGuard` (`canActivateChild` on the `MainComponent` route, so it covers lazy descendants) which reads the deepest `data.scope` on the route chain (so `cars/.../partajare` = personal and `.../sofer` = business narrow the cars default). Misfit -> redirect to `/main/cars` (there is no `/main/overview` route; unknown paths already fall to the garage). `orgRoles` applies only in business. UX only; the backend is authoritative. **A route with `feature` must repeat `scope` on the same `data` object** — the deepest-`.scope`-wins rule means a `feature` on a route whose own `data` has no `.scope` is invisible to the guard (see `core/car-page/FEATURE.md`).
- **Two extra `mainRoutes` children mount at the same path as `cars/`**: `` `${HAU_ROUTES.cars.path}/details` `` is used by both `../maintenance/maintenance.car-tab.routes` (Istoric/Plan) and `../reports/reports.car-tab.routes` (Rapoarte), alongside the `cars/` child itself — Angular's router backtracks to the next sibling route sharing a `path` when a candidate's own children fail to match the rest of the URL, which is what lets `cars/`'s lazy module (no longer defining those tab paths) fall through to whichever of these two actually has the requested tab. See "Tab routes move to their owning feature" in `core/car-page/FEATURE.md`.
- **Business visual indicator**: `AppComponent` toggles `body.ctx-business` from `SessionContextService.isBusiness()`; `theme/hau-design-system.scss` (end of file) overrides `--hau-primary*`, `--hau-on-dark-accent` and `--ion-color-primary*` for light and dark/bmw (see the mixins `hau-business-light/dark`; selectors are `html body.ctx-business.ctx-business` to beat the theme blocks). The sidebar's hard-coded orange rgba tokens are re-tinted in `main.component.scss` via `:host-context(body.ctx-business)`. The desktop sidebar badge is `ContextBadgeComponent` (`main/context-badge/`, in `.sidebar-top`); it navigates to `/auth/select-organization`. The mobile top strip (`.hau-ctx-strip`) was removed (user feedback: distracting); on mobile the info lives in `AccountDetailsComponent` (`main/account-details/`, selector `app-account-details`), rendered at the top of the Account card in `features/settings/settings.component.html`. It reads `BootstrapFacade.me$` + `SessionContextService` (org name/role; role label via `organization.role.<ROLE>`), outputs `logoutClick` (the host, `SettingsComponent.logout()`, resets car-list + bootstrap stores, disconnects the socket, logs out). The desktop sidebar's profile row already had an icon logout button and is unchanged.
- **Logout / context switch** (`logout()`, `switchToBusinessAccount()` here and in `SettingsComponent`) also call `BootstrapFacade.reset()` (new `BootstrapActions.Reset`) so a different login never reads the previous session's cached 5-minute bootstrap.
 Per-car expandable sub-menu: `visibleCars` + `carSubnav` `ng-template`, driven by `expandedCarId`/`toggleCarExpand`/`isCarExpanded`. The subnav's items past the hardcoded "Prezentare" button are no longer hardcoded either — `sidebarTabs` (`CarPageContributionsService.visibleSidebarTabs()`) and `goToSidebarTab()`/`isSidebarTabActive()` render whatever features registered/enabled a car-page tab (see `core/car-page/FEATURE.md`); a disabled feature's tab disappears from here the same way it disappears from the car hub's own tile grid.
- **Mobile bottom tab bar**: `.hau-bottom-tabs` in the template, rendered when `!hideBottomNav`. Items are `NavigationPreferencesService.resolvedBottomBar()` — a `ResolvedBottomBarEntry[]` (see `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`) — split into `bottomBarLeftItems`/`bottomBarRightItems` (`Math.ceil(length / 2)` on the left) around the `.tab-fab-spacer`, with the fixed Account tab (`/main/settings`) always last — `.tab-btn { flex: 1 }` means the markup/CSS needed no change for a variable item count, only the `@for` loop replacing the old two hardcoded buttons. `goToDestination(entry)`/`isDestinationActive(entry)` resolve a destination's navigation/active-state (Garage is special-cased for single-car mode; a `car`-kind entry goes straight to its own resolved `entry.carId` — no picker, see "No more car picker" in `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`). `hideBottomNav` is true for scoped-per-car routes (`isScopedCarRoute`) *except* `isScopedCarBottomBarRoute` (the single-car hub, or a car tab currently in the bar *for that slot's own car* — see "Bottom bar visibility on car-scoped routes" in `core/navigation/FEATURE.md`), the "Adaugă vehicul" form (`isCarFormRoute`), and the Journal write/edit form (`isBlogWriteRoute`) — the FAB (`.hau-fab`) is hidden/shown alongside the bar in every case. The FAB's actual action is registered per-page via `FabActionService`, not owned by this component — `fabAction.hasAction()` (a computed signal) drives `.hau-fab--disabled` / `aria-disabled` / the `nav.fab.disabled` aria-label on the template's own `<button>` when no page has one registered right now (see `core/fab-action.service.ts` and "FAB audit" below). The Account tab's icon wraps in `.tab-btn-icon-wrap` so `.tab-btn-badge` (unread count, capped at "9+") can sit on its corner; the count comes from `MainComponent.unreadNotifCount`, populated from `NotificationsFacade.unreadCount$`.
- **Notifications**: this component still owns loading them (`NotificationsFacade.load()`) and keeping the WebSocket connected (`NotificationsSocketService.connect()`/`.disconnect()`) for the whole authenticated session, since it wraps every route — but the list/accept/dismiss UI itself is `NotificationsPanelComponent` (`features/notifications/notifications-panel/` — its own vertical feature; see `features/notifications/FEATURE.md`), embedded here (desktop sidebar) and in `features/settings` (mobile Account screen); see that feature's FEATURE.md for the UI and the 2026-09-06 decision to move it there. The desktop sidebar also keeps a separate `attentionPanel` `ng-template` (upcoming document expiries, computed from `attentionItems`/`buildAttentionItems`) right below it — a different, older mechanism not tied to the `Notification` DB table, currently desktop-only.
- **No top-bar action buttons carry text** — every button projected into `.hau-header-end-actions` (via `HeaderActionsService`, per-page `#headerActionsTpl`), and every Cancel/Save-type button in a `<app-fullscreen-panel>`'s `fspStart`/`fspEnd` navbar slots (e.g. `add-maintenance-panel`), uses the shared `.hau-header-icon-btn` class (`theme/hau-design-system.scss`) — icon-only, round, 38×38, `box-sizing: border-box` + zeroed padding/margin so it lines up with `ion-back-button`. This is a hard rule as of 2026-09-05, not a per-page style choice. Only a page's own full-width in-content CTA (e.g. `add-maintenance-panel`'s bottom "Salvează intervenția" button) keeps a text label — that's form content, not the top bar.
- **Header buttons carry no painted background** — as of 2026-09-06, `ion-back-button` and any `.hau-header-icon-btn` projected into `.hau-header-end-actions` (`main.component.scss`) render as a bare icon only: no fill, border, blur, or shadow, on every route. Legibility comes entirely from `.hau-header--minimal::before`, a soft top-edge scrim behind the whole bar (Telegram-style), which is now unconditional — it used to only render once a page had a title (`.hau-header--has-title`, removed), so icon-only routes had nothing but the button's own fill for contrast; with that fill gone the scrim has to cover every route. The car hub root (`.hau-header--overlay`) gets its own variant of that scrim instead of the shared one: its hero photo can be any color, so a themed icon + themed scrim isn't reliable contrast — it fixes the icon to white and swaps the scrim for a plain black-to-transparent gradient with no blur (blurring the photo itself read worse than just darkening it).
- **One header structure app-wide, as of 2026-09-06** — every screen that used to hand-roll its own title/back-navigation now projects into this shared bar instead, so there's exactly one visual pattern (transparent floating bar, soft top scrim, icon-only round buttons) rather than several competing ones:
  - **Car hub root, SOLD/archived car**: `isCarHubRoot` alone used to force the overlay (photo-bleed, no title) treatment unconditionally; it's now gated by `MainComponent.isCarHubOverlay` (`isCarHubRoot && !headerActions.title()`). `cars-details.component.ts` already called `headerActions.setTitle()` for the SOLD case — it was just being ignored by the template guard. A SOLD car now gets the normal floating header (title + back button + theme-tinted scrim, real content clearance) instead of the old in-content `app-breadcrumb` + large `<h1>` block, which was a second, independent "where am I / go back" pattern. That block's now-orphaned code (`soldBreadcrumbItems`, `navigateToGarage()`, `.sv-title`) was removed along with it; the SOLD-only badge/subtitle/archived-date stay as ordinary content below the header.
  - **Notes and Sharing tabs** (`car-notes-page.component.ts`, `car-sharing-page.component.ts`): previously rendered their own title inline in content (`.cnp-header`/`.svp-header`) and never touched `HeaderActionsService` at all. Both now call `setTitle()`/`clearTitle()` in `ionViewWillEnter`/`ionViewWillLeave` like every other routed page; their panels (`CarNotesPanelComponent`, `ShareVehiclePanelComponent`) kept only the subtitle line in content, same convention as the rest of the app.
  - **`<app-fullscreen-panel>`** (`shared/component/fullscreen-panel/`): its navbar used to be a solid, sticky, opaque bar with a centered title — a second header system, deliberately separate because it needed its own stacking context for the full-viewport takeover. It's now visually the same pattern as the routed header (absolutely positioned over `.fsp-body`, transparent, the same `::before` scrim, `.hau-header-icon-btn` stripped of its fill via a scoped override) — the stacking-context reason for having a separate *component* still holds (unchanged), only its look was unified. `.fsp-body` reserves a fixed `14px + 38px + 14px + safe-area-top` clearance for it instead of the shell's `ResizeObserver`-measured one, since this navbar's content (one short fixed title, one icon button per side) never varies enough to need that. Fixed `car-maintenance-settings-panel`'s text-only "Close" button (`.cms-navbar-action`) to an icon-only `.hau-header-icon-btn` in the same pass — it had been the one leftover violation of the icon-only rule above.
  - **Left alone, deliberately**: `app-remove-car-panel` (a confirmation bottom sheet, not a page — see the safe-area rule in the frontend `CLAUDE.md` distinguishing bottom sheets from page headers) and `app-breadcrumb`'s other consumers (`documents-form`, `document-detail`, `cars-form`, `blog-entry-view`) — those already call `headerActions.setTitle()` for the real header and use the breadcrumb only as a supplementary in-content trail, not as a competing header.
- **Global vs. per-car routing**: per-car routes all live under `/main/cars/details/:id/...` (`MainComponent.CAR_DETAILS_PREFIX`, `scopedCarIdFromPath()`); a hardcoded `TOP_LEVEL_ROUTES` set drives whether the header shows a back button. The back button goes through `NavigationHistoryService.back()` (click captured in `MainComponent`); `backHref` is just `parentOf(currentPath)`, the fallback for a direct link. Reports, Maintenance-plan, and History are implemented once and reused both globally (`/main/reports`, `/main/maintenance`) and per-car (`/main/cars/details/:id/rapoarte`, `/plan`, `/istoric`).
- **Known gaps / decisions**:
  - Maintenance has a desktop sidebar entry but no mobile bottom-tab equivalent — reachable on mobile only via the per-car sub-menu or by navigating to `/main/maintenance` directly (e.g. from a notification). Not addressed by the 2026-09-05 nav change below.
  - The global (all-cars) Documents page has no dedicated mobile bottom-tab entry as of 2026-09-05 (see below) — its mobile entry points are the per-car Documents tab and a link from the Account/Settings screen (`settings.navigation.allDocuments`, `SettingsComponent.goToDocuments()`).
  - `HAU_ROUTES.dashboard` is a defined route constant with no actual route registered in `main.routes.ts` — dead, not in use.
  - `HAU_ROUTES.plan` (global maintenance plan) exists as a route but has no sidebar or bottom-tab entry — orphaned, not linked from navigation anywhere.
  - **2026-09-05**: added Account to the mobile bottom tab bar (previously header-only). Documents gave up its bar slot for it — reasoning: adding/viewing a document is always tied to one specific car (already covered by the per-car Documents tab), while checking aggregate spend/reports across all cars is a genuinely car-agnostic need, so Reports earned the global slot instead of Documents. (An earlier version of this change swapped out Reports instead — reverted same day once this reasoning came up.) Translation keys: `nav.tabs.account` (added), `nav.tabs.documente` (removed, no longer referenced). `settings.navigation.*` added for the new "All documents" link on the Account/Settings screen.
  - **2026-09-05 (later same day)**: removed the header-fallback Account button (`.hau-header-profile-btn`) entirely — now that Account lives in the bottom tab bar, the user decided it shouldn't be duplicated in the top header anywhere, including on the routes where the bottom bar itself is hidden (scoped-per-car screens, "Adaugă vehicul", Jurnal write/edit). Known consequence: on those specific routes there is currently no Account/Settings entry point at all (mobile); reachable only after navigating back to a screen with the bottom bar. Translation key `nav.profile` removed (orphaned). Same session: every per-page header action button (projected via `HeaderActionsService`) was also normalized to icon-only + round (`.hau-header-icon-btn` from `theme/hau-design-system.scss`) — no header action button carries a visible text label anymore.
  - **2026-09-06 fix**: `.hau-fab` was pinned at a flat `bottom: 26px`, not folding in `var(--ion-safe-area-bottom, 0px)` like `.hau-bottom-tabs` and every other bottom-anchored element in the app does — on Android devices using the classic 3-button on-screen nav (a taller inset than the gesture-nav pill), the FAB visually overlapped the system nav buttons. No new detection logic was needed: Capacitor's `SystemBars` plugin (`insetsHandling: 'css'`, see root `capacitor.config.ts`) already reports the actual on-screen nav bar height through that same CSS variable regardless of nav mode (gesture pill vs. 3-button bar) — `.hau-fab` just wasn't consuming it. Fixed to `bottom: calc(26px + var(--ion-safe-area-bottom, 0px))`, matching the established pattern (e.g. `blog-list.component.scss`'s fixed CTA).
  - **2026-09-06 fix — bottom of every mobile page was unreachable behind the fixed tab bar**: `.main-outlet` reserved clearance for `.hau-bottom-tabs` via `padding-bottom: 76px`. That padding had no actual effect: `<ion-router-outlet>` (and the `ion-page` it renders) is absolutely positioned with `inset: 0`, and per the CSS spec an absolutely-positioned element's containing block is the *padding* box of its positioned ancestor — so it ignores that ancestor's own padding and fills straight through it. Net effect: every routed page's scrollable content extended the full height of `.main-outlet`, with the last ~64-90px permanently covered by the fixed, opaque `.hau-bottom-tabs` and unreachable by scrolling — worst on pages with enough content to reach the bottom (first reported on the Settings screen, whose "Reminder-uri expirare" card and the new Account/logout card, see `features/settings/FEATURE.md`, sat right at that cutoff). Fixed by reserving the space with `margin-bottom` instead of `padding-bottom` (margin on a normal-flow flex item genuinely shrinks the space available to its absolutely-positioned child, unlike padding) and made it safe-area aware — `calc(64px + var(--ion-safe-area-bottom, 0px) + 12px)` — instead of the old flat guess, which didn't account for the tab bar growing taller on devices with a bottom inset. Added a `.main-outlet--no-bottom-nav` modifier class (bound to the same `hideBottomNav` getter that already hides `.hau-bottom-tabs`) so routes without the tab bar don't get a dead margin gap.
  - **2026-09-07 fix — tab bar/FAB crowded against the phone's own 3-button nav on some Android OEM builds, icons/labels bumped up**: the 2026-09-06 fix above assumed `--ion-safe-area-bottom` always reports the true on-screen nav bar height, any nav mode. On at least one real device (classic 3-button nav, MIUI/HyperOS-family) that assumption didn't hold — Capacitor's `SystemBars` plugin reads the inset from `WindowInsetsCompat.Type.systemBars()` (a well-formed request that does include `navigationBars()`), but some OEM builds are known to under-report that value regardless, leaving no built-in fallback. Symptom: the tab bar and FAB rendered with little to no clearance above the real nav buttons. Mitigated with a floor, not a fix for the misreport itself (no device/logcat access to confirm the exact reported value): `body.hau-native .hau-bottom-tabs` and `body.hau-native .hau-fab` now use `max(var(--ion-safe-area-bottom, 0px), 48px)` instead of the raw variable — 48px approximates a typical 3-button nav bar height, and `max()` is a no-op on devices already reporting a correct, larger inset. Scoped to `body.hau-native` specifically (not applied unconditionally) because `--ion-safe-area-bottom` is legitimately `0px` on web by design (see `global.scss`) and forcing a 48px floor there would add unwanted empty space in the browser/PWA. Separately, tab icon/label sizes were bumped (`1.3rem → 1.5rem` icon, `0.64rem → 0.72rem` label) for legibility — unrelated to the inset issue, just judged too small at the original size. If crowding is still reported after this on a real device, the 48px floor undershoots the actual inset gap on that device/OEM, and the next step would be a native-side fix in `MainActivity.java` cross-checking `WindowInsetsCompat` against the `navigation_bar_height` Android resource — needs live device/logcat access to scope correctly, not attempted here.
  - **2026-09-08 fix — labels still overflowed into Android's 3-button bar in landscape**: the safe-area padding above was correct, but `.hau-bottom-tabs` still declared a flat `height: 64px`. The app's global `box-sizing: border-box` makes padding part of that height, so a large landscape three-button inset consumed the tab row instead of extending the bar; icons and labels then overflowed outside its box. The bar height is now `calc(64px + effective-bottom-inset)` (including the native 48px floor), while `padding-bottom` remains the inset. This preserves a 64px tab-content row entirely above the system navigation area and stays consistent with `.main-outlet`'s reserved clearance.
